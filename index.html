PhD work sample · Poster

When linkage disequilibrium challenges causal inference

Supervisors: Dr Sjoerd Beentjes and Prof Chris Ponting · Advisor: Dr Ava Khamseh

In plain termsNearby DNA variants are inherited together, so it is hard to tell which one actually affects a trait. I test which causal estimators still give honest answers when that happens.

Six estimators compared on simulated genotypes (10,000 people, 1,000 variants, 200 replicates per scenario) across strong, moderate and minimal linkage disequilibrium, run as array jobs on the Eddie HPC cluster.

What I found
  • Confidence interval coverage depends on how strongly variants are linked, not on how the outcome was generated.
  • Wang-CTMLE performs best under strong and moderate linkage disequilibrium, with C-TMLE₁ and scalable C-TMLE close behind.
  • Standard TMLE underperforms throughout, because it underestimates its own variance rather than because it is biased.

Health and clinical data

Has the deprivation gap in hospital admissions changed since COVID?

People in Scotland's most deprived areas have about twice as many emergency admissions as those in the least deprived. Using Public Health Scotland open data for every NHS board, I measure that gap four ways and test whether the pandemic shifted it. The gap narrowed from 2.13 to 2.01 times between 2019/20 and 2024/25, at about its pre-pandemic pace, and is now widest in Lothian and Greater Glasgow.

R, tidyverse, slope and relative indexes of inequality, interrupted time series, Shiny running in the browser

Causal inference methods

When does adjusting for more make things worse?

Covariates that only affect who gets treated make standard causal estimators unstable. Across 500 simulated datasets run on the Eddie HPC cluster, C-TMLE was 2.5 times more accurate than AIPW when those covariates were strong, close to an oracle that knew which covariates mattered.

Julia, TMLE.jl, C-TMLE, Pluto, HPC (SGE array jobs)

Business and consumer analytics

Did the email campaign actually work?

When a campaign targets its best customers, a naive comparison overstated its impact by about 60%. Checked against a randomised experiment on 64,000 customers, AIPW and TMLE, implemented from scratch, recover the true effect with honest confidence intervals. A causal forest trained only on the targeted data then picks out who responds most, and the experiment confirms its ranking.

Python, scikit-learn, EconML, causal inference, simulation

Who should the bank call?

On 41,188 real telemarketing calls, targeting the model's top 30% of clients wins 2.5 times as many term-deposit subscriptions as random calling on the same budget, and keeps 93% of the profit with 70% fewer calls. Trained on earlier calls and tested on later ones, the model still reaches a ROC-AUC of 0.74.

Python, XGBoost, random forest, SHAP, out-of-time validation, business case modelling

MSc thesis · AIMS Ghana · June 2024

Semiparametric estimation techniques protect against model misspecification bias

Supervisor: Dr Sjoerd Beentjes · 46 pages · R

In plain termsIf a statistical model makes the wrong assumptions, its estimate of a treatment's effect can be confidently wrong. I tested whether newer "semiparametric" methods avoid that trap, first on simulated data where the true answer was known, then on a real public health trial.
Methods and findings
  • Compared three traditional estimators (difference in means, linear regression and inverse probability weighting) with two semiparametric ones: one-step estimation and targeted maximum likelihood estimation (TMLE).
  • In simulation, one-step estimation had the lowest bias at every sample size and kept coverage close to 95%. Coverage for regression and weighting collapsed towards zero as samples grew.
  • TMLE beat the traditional estimators on variance but kept a small bias and lost coverage in large samples. I traced this to fitting the models and the targeting step on the same data, which cross-validated TMLE is designed to fix.
  • On data from the WASH Benefits Bangladesh trial (water, sanitation, handwashing and nutrition interventions and child growth), the two semiparametric methods gave confidence intervals about half as wide as the traditional ones.

Solving the FitzHugh–Nagumo model with Python

Supervisor: Dr Gaston E. Awashie · Group thesis with Jennifer Owusua Debrah, Fritz Effah Amoakohene and Emmanuel Korsah · 52 pages · Python

In plain termsMany sudden cardiac deaths are caused by a breakdown in the electrical signal that keeps the heart beating. The FitzHugh–Nagumo model describes that signal with two equations. We reviewed how the heart's electrical activity is modelled, then solved the model numerically in Python.
Methods and findings
  • Reviewed models of cardiac electrical activity, from the detailed ionic models (Hodgkin–Huxley, Luo–Rudy, Fox) to simpler ones (FitzHugh–Nagumo, Aliev–Panfilov).
  • Covered the numerical methods behind the solution: Euler, Runge–Kutta, finite differences and Newton–Raphson.
  • Solved the two-equation system with SciPy's ODEINT and plotted how the shape, amplitude and timing of the signal evolve.
